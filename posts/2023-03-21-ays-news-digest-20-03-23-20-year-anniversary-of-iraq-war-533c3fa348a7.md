#### LITHUANIA
### Sienos Grupe need volunteers from April onwards!

Sienos Grupe is an NGO providing humanitarian aid on the Lithuanian border. They further help asylum seekers to find jobs and a place to live, collect data on pushbacks on the Lithuanian-Belarussian border, provide practical and mental support to asylum seekers in the detention centres, search for missing persons, and advocate for more humane policies.

They ask for a minimum of 2 weeks — you can find out more here: [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/photo/?fbid=585548083610334&set=a.464122172419593)
#### CZECH REPUBLIC
### Integration of Ukrainian refugees less expensive than expected in Czech Republic

Ukrainian refugees are the largest immigration influx in the Czech Republic. Despite far-right criticism and a growing nationalist movement, the Czech Republic has supported the integration of Ukrainian refugees through [providing free accommodation, access to labour market, and access to schools for children.](https://www.infomigrants.net/en/post/47598/well-integrated-ukrainian-refugees-in-the-czech-republic?preview=1679310145176&fbclid=IwAR2ovmher5dnR31HbKZUXlSIOuk9LZ_yIHnex8-9ZhW0rFpT5jFTVtoQSak)

The government admits there were problems but the longterm impact on the economy has been much less than initially expected. This is due to the fact many Ukrainians are now in work, paying taxes and therefore contributing to society.


> But the state doesn’t only have expenditures — it also profits from those Ukrainians who have found work and pay Czech taxes. That has allowed the Czech government to take in about €350 million in tax revenue from Ukrainian laborers. 





> “Costs for Ukrainian refugees will be lower than initially anticipated,” says Minister of Finance Zbynek Stanjura. Refugees integrated into the Czech labor market quicker than expected and have been less of a burden on the health care system than feared. Furthermore, Czech citizens have been generous when it comes to sheltering Ukrainian refugees free of charge. 




#### GERMANY
### Government under pressure to restart deportations to Afghanistan which were suspended in 2021

Political pressure has meant that the Government is currently debating whether Afghan asylum seekers who have committed crimes and/or are seen as a threat to national security should get deported back to Afghanistan.


> “If people from abroad do not comply with our legal system, but commit criminal offenses and thus endanger our security, they have to leave our country.” — Stephan Thomae, from the liberal FDP party 





Pro Asyl have commented on this debate, claiming that deportations would mean communicating and working with the Taliban, the exact regime that forced so many people to flee. Furthermore, deportation can only take place if there is no risk of torture, death or persecution.

[Germany considering forced returns to Afghanistan: reports — InfoMigrants](https://www.infomigrants.net/en/post/47617/germany-considering-forced-returns-to-afghanistan-reports)
#### UK
### Private firms profiting from housing asylum seekers

There are currently 395 hotels being used across the UK to house asylum seekers. Hotel owners are told to hand over their properties to outsourced companies, and according to Home Office reports, there are 3 companies currently responsible for housing asylum seekers; [Serco, Mears Group, and Calder Conferences.](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-64991234.amp)

The BBC analysed annual accounts of the three companies, looking at incoming revenue from the Home Office and turnover/profits made:


> Court documents have revealed Mears Group is running 80 hotels in north-east England, Scotland and Northern Ireland. According to its annual report, the company increased its annual revenue by 22% in 2021. 





> Leeds-based Calder’s annual accounts for the year ending February 2022 show turnover increased from £5.98m to £23.66m. The firm’s pre-tax profits trebled, from £2.1m to £6.3m. 





> Calder’s director, Debbie Hoban, saw her annual remuneration increase from £230,000 to £2.2m. 





At the same time, there has been an increase in the use of temporary accommodation to house asylum seekers.
