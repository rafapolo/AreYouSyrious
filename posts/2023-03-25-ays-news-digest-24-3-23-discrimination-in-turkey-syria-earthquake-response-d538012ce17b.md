#### Discrimination in Turkey-Syria earthquake response


![Photo Credit: [Dr. Apostolos Veizis](https://twitter.com/AVeizis) , a temporary shelter in Greece.](../assets/d538012ce17b/0*QFr2ZpV6IWcSmuQa)

Photo Credit: [Dr. Apostolos Veizis](https://twitter.com/AVeizis) , a temporary shelter in Greece.

Following the devastating earthquake in February this year, a report has been published on the discrimination involved in how victims in the area are being treated.


> “The Refugee Rights Platform prepared a report on the situation of Syrians in the earthquake region. According to the report, which was made public on 21 March, the Day Against Racism, toilets, clean water and healthy food are still the biggest problems for Syrians… It was stated that the Syrians who stayed in the earthquake zone were the poorest and that they could not afford to eat or buy water.” [Marxist.org](https://marksist-org.translate.goog/icerik/Haber/19379/Suriyelilerin-kaybedecek-hicbir-seyleri-kalmamis,-sadece-ilkel-cadirlari-var?fbclid=IwAR1_Kc8f2OdHVINi98FKgxMmim_ji7JY9W_t42LaTUlPJvwKpy3608itU3o&_x_tr_sl=tr&_x_tr_tl=en&_x_tr_hl=en-US&_x_tr_pto=wapp) 




The initial response of Western nations in providing physical and financial support was swift and public, yet people on the Syrian side of the border were already in need of humanitarian assistance due to the on-going civil war. This also meant that gaining access to those affected was difficult.

The civil war in Syria, which has been being fought since 2011, has meant that access to areas in humanitarian need has been blocked by restricting which borders may be used for important items needed during a humanitarian response. [The Russian regime, which supports Damascus](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/aussenpolitik/laenderinformationen/syrien-node/-/2572542) , has been assisting in blocking access through the Security Council, and used its veto in 2022 to curtail the validity of a certain agreement, complicating importations during winter.

However, this report comes from activists on the ground in Turkey who observed how NGOs and government workers interact with those in need of aid. Half of all Syrian refugees in Turkey reside in the earthquake zone, many are extremely poor and can’t afford to buy bread or water. A lack of tents and toilet facilities are also of grave concern, whilst application to stay in a container system seems to have been accepted by Turkish citizens, with Syrians still waiting for a response.

The cruellest example of discrimination mentioned in this report is of cotton candy being withheld from a three-year-old child because it didn’t speak Turkish.

#### SEA and SAR
#### One hundred ninety people rescued in a collaborative effort

Thanks to Alarmphone, MSF and rescue vessel Louise Michel — [190 people were rescued](https://www.infomigrants.net/en/post/47753/msf-rescues-190-migrants-off-italian-coast?fbclid=IwAR3A8hLOb_p1Y5krCauAw2Vmj4hp0WSqlP3QuMnVPTA6NqE_oyUBE1kI30M) from a ship in the Mediterranean. The Italian authorities have issued a port of safety two and a half day’s sail away from the current position — consistent with the new law to hinder rescue organisations. [Louise Michel](https://twitter.com/MVLouiseMichel/status/1639364129252167682?s=20&fbclid=IwAR3rDf7f4-gTvTnEHwNeR7BcNdKwEwZBsvgiHYVMXhrIohUy9xJQQG-X394) also undertook other rescues before helping MSF, and currently have 116 people on board.

#### UNITED KINGDOM
#### Climate visas proposed by conservative think tank

Legal routes should be created for those whose livelihood is being affected by climate change to come legally to the UK to fill skilled-worker roles. This is the proposal of Think-Tank Onward, whose co-founder Will Tanner recently became Prime Minister Rishi Sunak’s deputy chief of staff.

This proposal to make safe and legal routes for some people seems completely absurd given the recent anti-refugee bill. As reported in this [News Digest](https://medium.com/are-you-syrious/ays-news-digest-10-03-23-stopthebill-f72af2d4fdc7) , there are attempts to make it impossible for those arriving by ‘irregular’ means to find safety in the UK.

UNHCR has published a report into the legal implications of the bill, which is being accelerated through the various legal stages in the UK Parliament. Some particularly concerning parts of the report are below, whilst [the whole report can be read here.](https://www.unhcr.org/641c7cfea?fbclid=IwAR0Au-fzzhSC1nbB_sl2wUyxEKZYst6X8eTLWxpuj587N8PiCQNcypdqkfI)


> “4. The Bill, if enacted, would breach the UK’s obligations under the Refugee Convention, the 1954 Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons, the 1961 Convention for the Reduction of Statelessness and international human rights law and would significantly undermine the international refugee protection system. 





> “13. Those who meet the conditions for the asylum ban, including children and families, will be liable to detention in the UK for as long as the Secretary of State considers reasonable. Judicial oversight will be significantly curtailed, and statutory limits on the detention of children and pregnant women disapplied. Without time limits on detention and given the limited prospects of removal, there is a real risk that asylum-seekers, refugees and stateless people, including children and vulnerable adults, will be subject to indefinite detention in violation of international law” [UNHCR Report](https://www.unhcr.org/641c7cfea?fbclid=IwAR0Au-fzzhSC1nbB_sl2wUyxEKZYst6X8eTLWxpuj587N8PiCQNcypdqkfI)

ECRE and PICUM [have published](https://ecre.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/PIC-ECR-Rights-and-EU-funds-March-2023.pdf) a joint policy note regarding the use of funds for supporting people seeking asylum, which covers inclusion, migration and asylum as well as border security.

The paper highlights the need for local and civil organisations in individual countries to have a say in how funds are best spent. [The full report can be read here.](https://ecre.org/ecre-and-picum-policy-note-fundamental-rights-compliance-of-funding-supporting-migrants-asylum-applicants-and-refugees-inside-the-european-union/?fbclid=IwAR2qBs-zWxZoLUN2Kuqj9_OoxVDjgG6R2Ih-OA5n7hhybPaM1PEMTyQs4Jg)

An [international smuggling network has reportedly been dismantled](https://twitter.com/rgowans/status/1639332435249725445?s=20&fbclid=IwAR0OVQRftvU1jurOYPJmkDf1Zb2BvSsFo3TxlhX7sXxqUa2_N6NK1yaXlMA) by Algerian authorities. According to Algerian media, the network was involved in moving people from Syria and Lebanon via Libya and Algeria to Europe. Eleven people were arrested after [a five-month operation.](https://www.africanews.com/2023/03/23/algeria-dismantles-migrant-smuggling-network-to-europe/)

In some more positive news, things for migrant workers may be easier. Proposed changes to the Single Permit Directive would make it easier for people on work permits to change jobs. This thread explains how and way it’s good news.
