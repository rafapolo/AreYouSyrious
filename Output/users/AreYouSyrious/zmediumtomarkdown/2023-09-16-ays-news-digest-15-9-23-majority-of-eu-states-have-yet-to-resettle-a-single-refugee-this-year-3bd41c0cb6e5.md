### EU Observer claims most EU states have not resettled a single individual this year

In a time of increasing need for unity and for humanitarian protection for asylum seekers and refugees, the resettlement of refugees across the EU is diminishing.


> …Austria, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Greece, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, and Slovenia. 





> None of them resettled in the first half of this year. Malta took in one person, according to figures provided by the UNHCR. 





The IRC claim the EU is far behind in their aim of resettling 16,000 individuals by the end of 2023— [so far only 6,000 individuals have been resettled](https://www.rescue.org/eu/press-release/ahead-fridays-deadline-irc-urges-eu-leaders-urgently-ramp-refugee-resettlement#:~:text=The%20International%20Rescue%20Committee%20%28IRC%29%20urges%20leaders%20to,number%20more%20proportionate%20to%20Europe%E2%80%99s%20wealth%20and%20size.) . They are further demanding that 44,000 individuals be resettled in 2024, and another 48,000 in 2025.

Last year, the EU resettled roughly 16,500 individuals, when they pledged 20,000 — the majority going to Germany (4,700), Sweden (4,500) and France (3,100). By this time last year, the EU was in a similar situation, where the vast majority of countries had yet to resettle a single individual.

In addition, as of July 2023, Sweden has only resettled 200 individuals — a huge decrease. This is partially as a result of increased anti-migrant sentiments, and the fact they have stopped taking refugees for resettlement from Syria and Iraq.


> _As negotiations continue on the EU’s proposed asylum and migration reforms, leaders should be able to agree on at least one thing: refugee resettlement benefits everyone. It is a desperately-needed lifeline for some of the world’s most vulnerable refugees. For the poor and middle-income countries hosting 76% of the world’s refugees, resettlement is a valuable demonstration of solidarity. And for receiving countries, including EU states, it provides a humane, structured, legal and orderly approach to welcoming people in need._ 





> _“For every pledge that remains unfulfilled, another resettlement place is wasted — leaving people trapped in limbo, playing an eternal waiting game, in a country that cannot support their needs. The IRC and other humanitarian organisations are continuing to call on states to meet their target of resettling 15,897 refugees by the end of this year. We believe it’s perfectly achievable for them to jointly welcome 44,000 people via this safe route in 2024, increasing this goal in years to come in reflection of Europe’s true capacity to welcome which is clearly much higher.” —_ **Harlem Désir, IRC’s Senior Vice President, Europe**

### Lawsuit filed by survivors of the Pylos shipwreck

Following on from [AYS Digest 13/9/23](ays-news-digest-13-9-23-italian-authorities-risk-lives-of-44-people-ca3f8b479bd7) , [40 survivors have filed a lawsuit against the Greek authorities, claiming they failed to save the lives on board.](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2023/9/14/new-lawsuit-filed-against-greece-on-behalf-of-refugee-shipwreck-survivors?fbclid=IwAR3rg7b9IruGxMkNOLXmJDRGsD7kGw77o-qIhxdMv4Ta7Qfs-txFbJVlUiI) A total of 82 bodies were found, 104 people were rescued and hundreds are still missing.

Survivors allege that the boat was subjected to towing before it capsized and sunk. The coastguard admitted to attaching a rope to the boat but denied towing the boat and causing any wrongdoing.

The media have also discovered that survivor testimonies were tampered with and that there were other coastguard boats that could have been mobilised to prevent the shipwreck.

#### DENMARK
### Asylum system disadvantages Syrian women, putting them at greater risk of detention and deportation

Denmark recently won international praise for their policy that enabled Afghan women to automatically receive refugee status due to the Taliban’s persecution of women and girls. However, the asylum system puts Syrian women at a huge disadvantage compared to their male counterparts. Many have experienced abuse and discrimination in both Syria and Denmark, and are being failed by the asylum system.

In 2019, the Danish government categorised the area around Damascus as ‘safe for return’. Consequently, hundreds of asylum seekers lost their Danish residency permits. A large proportion were women, as they often had the weakest form of protection. Men of fighting age risked military conscription in Syria, so were given full protection under the Refugee Convention.

Denmark does not have diplomatic relations with Syria, so people who lose their permits are sent to dangerous deportation camps, where they are separated from their community and are not allowed to work or study.


> We found that 64 per cent of Syrians given the weakest form of protection in Denmark are women, putting them at significantly greater risk of return. Since 2019, more than 1,000 Syrian refugees have had their permits reassessed and more than 150 have lost final appeals, of whom more than 70 are women.

#### UK
### Keir Starmer fails to promote safe passage for asylum seekers in proposed immigration policy announcement

[Starmer continues to mirror the inhumane, divisive language and policy used by the Conservatives](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2023/sep/14/slogans-keir-starmer-refugee-charity-staff-labour-new-immigration-policy?CMP=share_btn_tw&fbclid=IwAR0JHLrgA2DFEqO7M0EPkTSaFnFX0TfGEeUPrGMHyWP_5VKTAIxLwAwdLq4) . Steve Smith, the CEO of Care4Calais, argues Starmer could have used this opportunity to finally discuss the possibility of creating humanitarian visas for refugees that could be applied for anywhere in the world. Instead, however, he continues the same rhetoric as Rishi Sunak. Neither of them have any understanding of the refugee crisis and how to actually implement a humane approach that would reduce crossings and the role of smugglers.


> Nothing will stop the crossings and the smugglers apart from humanitarian visas for refugees, which can be applied for from anywhere in the world. 





> People have fled some very big things in their home countries and UK government deterrent policies won’t prevent them from trying to reach safety. Those who want to come here often speak the language or have relatives here and many of them say to me that they see the UK as a country that upholds human rights. The only way to stop the crossings and the smugglers is by providing refugees with safe passage. 





> **But unfortunately, the asylum seekers here have become pawns in the general election game and their humanity and individual stories have been lost. —** Imogen Hardman, an operations manager in refugee camps in Northern France
