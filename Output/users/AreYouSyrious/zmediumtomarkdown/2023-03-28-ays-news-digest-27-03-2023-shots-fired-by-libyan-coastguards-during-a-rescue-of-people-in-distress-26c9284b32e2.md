#### FEATURE
### The faces of the border: more deaths, gunshots on rescuing activists and criminalisation of solidarity

Borders are not natural. They are constructed as a result of a dynamic process that can involve media, politics, geography and so on. Borders have many faces.

First face: a sea, a wall, a river, a mountain, a forest. Natural geography is used to define the physical boundaries between nation states. Thus the Mediterranean Sea becomes the enemy of those seeking to reach Europe. The sea becomes passively complicit in European border management policies.

The death of 29 Sub-Saharan people close to the Tunisian coast is not a ‘tragedy’: it is the consequence of years of externalisation of European borders. At least four boats suffered a shipwreck over the past days, while anti-migrant discourses and racist hate increase in Tunisia.

The atmosphere in the country is getting increasingly tense for Sub-Saharan people. Staying in the country is less and less an option. Nevertheless European governments continue to follow externalisation policies as the ‘solution’: increasing funds to countries close to the borders of Europe (and beyond), as well as constant support through the provision of new technologies and means of border controls.

[Read more here](https://www.infomigrants.net/en/post/47782/29-migrants-found-dead-off-tunisias-coast-as-mood-against-migrants-turns-sour?fbclid=IwAR1-7LP15B9ERgS22BmJn7h-kfeEV2gsorZ4rWQhXXAXjkZRQcMUhBYgV0k) about the shipwreck.

It seems that Giorgia Meloni, Italy’s prime minister, forgets that human lives are involved when, with regard to the situation in Tunisia, she expresses her concerns at Tunisia’s “serious financial problems” that could spark a “migratory wave”. It is not surprising then that European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen stated that the EU is ready to mobilize €110 million to North African countries, including Tunisia. At which aim? In order to prevent people crossing the sea.

A kind of (willed) blindness stubbornly leads European governments to fail to see the reality from which people are fleeing. Or they simply do not seem to care.

However it matters little whether the person crossing the border escapes from a dangerous situation or not. The fact is that he takes the decision to risk his life by crossing a border that cannot be crossed safely, simply because he was born in a certain context and with a certain passport.

And here it is the other face of the border: the border as an act. An act of power to establish clear neo-colonialist hierarchies.

The choice of European governments to support governments that actively violate human rights is a decisive act that is creating ever stronger and more dangerous borders. The EU and member states have supported and trained the Libyan coastguard, which push back people on the move stopped at sea to detention centres.

A new report by an investigator from a UN fact-finding mission is expected to be presented later this week to the UN Human Rights Council. The focus of the report will be the involvement and responsibility of the EU in crimes against people migrating in which Libya is involved. [Read more here](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/libyan-migrants-are-victims-crimes-against-humanity-un-mission-2023-03-27/?fbclid=IwAR1rVsPkuSyf4Lo9cn9e93QWu2q3SsftK8qR9TtpQAILS86_9UAsQ_WdOLI)

Nothing new. 
The Italian government has been actively supporting Libya for years. Despite explicit evidence of violence and torture by the Libyan authorities.

But the border is also this. Violence. 
A constant act of physical and psychological violence. Here is another of the myriad faces of the border

To the extreme of violence. Shooting those who are already in danger. This is what has recently happened.


[![](https://thecivilfleet.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/230325_dc325_christiangohdes_71a6979.jpg)](https://thecivilfleet.wordpress.com/2023/03/26/eu-supported-libyan-coastguards-use-guns-to-threaten-refugee-rescuers/?fbclid=IwAR2XT55kwcimocdDYjAt5yBSSeAh2yni7_woppxx0TD1sKceKFPjgX6fIC4)


Last Saturday, while the Ocean Viking (an SOS Mediterranee rescue ship) was trying to save 80 people in distress, the Libyan coastguard approached the rescue ship.

According to the SOS Mediterranee crew the Libyan authorities :


> “started behaving aggressively, threatening with guns and firing gunshots in the air. (…) As the safety of the crew was under threat, the Ocean Viking sailed away from the scene at full speed, while the Libyan Coastguard continued to fire shots” 





And they denounced this…


> “… escalation of violence and the deliberate compromising of our crew and shipwrecked people’s safety in the central Mediterranean by the EU-sponsored Libyan coastguard”.

This is not the first time that rescue activities have been hindered. On the contrary: it is now a systematic practice. NGOs active in sea rescues are daily confronted not only with the kind of physical violence described above, but also with a more insidious and structural violence: legal violence on the part of institutions.

One more face of the borders: criminalisation of solidarity.

This is what happened the past few days to the Rescue Vessel [Louise Michel](https://twitter.com/hashtag/LouiseMichel?src=hashtag_click) , that was detained on 25th of March after the rescue of 180 people. The Louise Michel rescue crew stated on twitter:


> “During the Disembarkation on Lampedusa in the early morning, we were already informed that our ship is being detained due to violation of the new Italian decree”

#### GREECE
### €18.5 million funds approved to purchase new border vessels


> “Through this action, the operational capacity of the Hellenic Coast Guard will be strengthened, which will contribute to the control and monitoring of illegal migration flows, the protection of human life and the fight against crime at our sea borders” 





With these words, reported by [InfoMigrants](https://www.infomigrants.net/en/post/47760/greece-approves-funding-for-new-coast-guard-vessels?fbclid=IwAR3DZpr2aaaY3iOp4nHzwoGL0hYvXcrBQQ9ZQXNYCVBGXqor0qxWHh1_tYQ) , the Ministry of Migration and Asylum explained the new investment in border controls.

€18.5 million spent on securing borders: a further step to make the journey of those who try to reach safety in Greece and Europe more difficult and risky.
#### MALTA
### Protest after 4 years of El Hiblu3 trial, in which three teenagers are accused of terrorism because they refused to be pushed back to Libya

It was 26th March of 2019. 108 people were about to drown in the Mediterranean Sea, but ‘thankfully’ they were saved in time. ‘Thankfully’, one could say. However, once the cargo ship _El Hiblu 1_ rescued them _,_ it was instructed to bring the people back to Libya. Libya: a country with no future for them, a country responsible for torturing people migrating. A return to Libya for most people was (and it still is) a not so preferable alternative to death.

Three African teenagers peacefully protested against refoulement in Libya and got their cargo to go in the direction of Malta. When they reached Malta, they were detained for seven months. And after four years they risk being charged of terrorism.

Refugees in Libya gathered to protest against the charges in front of the Maltese court

#### POLAND
### Hunger strike in detention center in Krosno Odrzanskie

No translations, no legal help, no psychological help. Krosno Odrzanskie is located 50km from the German border and it is one of the six detention centres in Poland. People there have no idea what to expect, they don’t receive any information about their legal status. Therefore, as reported by the No Borders Team, 26 people detained in Krosno Odrzanskie decided to start an hunger strike in order to demand information about their detention.
