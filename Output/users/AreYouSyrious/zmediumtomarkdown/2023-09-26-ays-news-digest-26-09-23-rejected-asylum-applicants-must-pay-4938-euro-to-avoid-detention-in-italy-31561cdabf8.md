#### FEATURE
### The sum of 4938 EURO to be free

Many people reaching Italy had no choice but to pay smugglers to leave their country of origin, since there are no safe paths. Once they reach Europe, they have the right to ask for asylum. However, under the Cutro Decree, the decision about the request is made very quickly. So that whoever is coming from a so called “safe country” is forced to go in a CPR, a pre-removal reception centre. Those centres are notorious for human rights violations and have been been denounced many times by NO CPR.

It is an unending journey: there are no alternatives but to pay to be free. This is what was established by the new Decree on 22nd September. The rule will apply to people who have eluded or tried to elude controls and come from a ‘safe’ country. [Almost 5,000 euro](https://www.quotidiano.net/cronaca/migranti-5mila-euro-cpr-richidenti-asilo-kvv9yb6n?fbclid=IwAR1WQtIYS3FV_tiv2zeJbkgyaPFIkNtJhKPYZz-JtOMB_prUdWpLCxT07zU) will be needed for an asylum seeker as an alternative to avoid remaining in the repatriation centre until the outcome of his appeal against the rejection of his application. According to the Cutro Decree, anyone arriving in Italy goes through an accelerated process of analysing their asylum application if coming from a what is considered to be a safe country. Tunisia, Algeria, Cape Verde, Ivory Coast, Gambia, Ghana, Morocco, Nigeria and Senegal: for people coming from those countries there will be an accelerated ‘border’ procedure, while waiting for his/her application to be processed. The ‘fast-track’ procedure should lead to an answer on the asylum application within 28 days after the application. Paying 4938 euro would allow migrant people to avoid this 28-day detention in administrative detention centres.

#### TUNISIA
### The EU externalisation process continues: funds to Tunisia are being released to ‘halt irregular immigration’

€127 million: this is the amount that the EU Commission approved to be given to Tunisia. Of those funds, 67 million are allocated to the Tunisian Coast Guard and Navy. The externalisation of European borders is forcing people who want to reach Europe to stay in a risky country such as Tunisia.

Those funds will be used for ships, thermal imaging cameras, radars and other surveillance technologies, which will make the journey to the EU more dangerous for people on the move who have no choice but to take irregular paths, since there are no safe and legal ways to reach the EU. Visas are really difficult, and in many countries impossible to obtain.

The deal between the EU and Tunisia was established last July in order to halt “irregular migration”. Last week, funds started to be released to Tunisia, while Italy’s far right government is claiming there is a crisis of arrivals in the country. However, the situation in Italy is only the culmination (perhaps) of poor reception management, where facilities are not prepared to welcome and support those arriving in the country.

Many people arriving these days on Lampedusa are fleeing from Tunisia, where hate speech and violent actions towards people from the Subsaharan region are politically accepted and have even been fuelled for months.

#### HUNGARY
### Council of Europe press release expresses concern over pushbacks of asylum seekers

Pushbacks from Hungary are increasing, while no measures seem to have been taken to respect human rights at the border with Serbia. According to the Commitee, Hungary must achieve tangible progress by September 2024 in order to avoid the Committee of Ministers taking “new action” to ensure it abides by its obligations.


> “The Committee of Ministers reiterates “grave concern” that despite the authorities’ repeated indications that asylum system reform is underway, no information on concrete measures has been communicated and collective expulsions not only continue, but their numbers are reportedly increasing at a “concerning rate.” According to the Hungarian National Police, 158,565 pushbacks to Serbia were reported in 2022. Up to mid-September 2023, the number already stood at approximately 66,000"

#### POLAND
### Another victim of borders

A 20-year-old Somali asylum seeker was buried in Minsk yesterday. She died after being pushed back twice at the Polish-Belarusian EU external border

#### UK
### Concerns over asylum seekers’ medical assistance in Essex

A young man, probably from Iran, was on hunger strike to protest against the harsh conditions at the Wethersfield camp in Essex when he fell ill. However, he did not receive immediate medical attention.
