#### FEATURE

The Opinion on the compatibility with European standards of Italian Decree Law №1 of 2 January 2023 on the management of migratory flows, that [we wrote about previously](ays-news-digest-9-01-23-trial-of-human-rights-defenders-in-lesvos-begins-e986700cc7e0) , scrutinises Italian decisions made entirely without consultations or considerations of NGO sector experts in the field.

The Opinion was prepared by Expert Council on NGO Law member Carla Ferstman.

Its recommendations are:


> **The Italian Government should proceed with effective consultations with civil society groups most affected (particularly search and rescue NGOs) prior to taking any steps to translate Decree Law №1/2023 into a formal piece of legislation.** 





> **Furthermore, the Government should revoke it until such time that such consultations can take place, and until adequate and effective steps are taken to ensure that migrants’ lives are not being put at risk by the inability of search and rescue NGOs to work effectively.** 





> **Any future law to be enacted should be in line with European standards.** 





Find the entire Opinion [**here**](https://rm.coe.int/expert-council-conf-exp-2023-opinion-italy-30-jan-2023-en/1680a9fe26) .

Furthermore, the CoE is condemning the Decree as well.
### Letter from Commissioner Dunja Mijatović


> “The decree and the practice of assigning distant ports for the disembarkation of people rescued at sea risk depriving people in distress of life-saving assistance from NGOs on the Mediterranean’s deadliest migration route.” 




With these reasons, Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights Dunja Mijatović wrote a letter to Interior Minister Matteo Piantedosi asking him to withdraw the anti-NGO decree adopted by the Meloni government, which, among other things, includes a ban on multiple rescues, as [reported](https://www.lastampa.it/esteri/2023/02/02/news/il_consiglio_deuropa_scrive_a_piantedosi_il_decreto_anti-ong_va_ritirato-12618403/) .

According to the Council of Europe, these provisions “could **hinder NGO search and rescue operations and thus be contrary to Italy’s obligations under human rights and international law** .” Not the least because “by complying with this provision, NGO commanders would effectively be failing in their rescue obligations under international law.”

Regarding the practice of designating locations in central and northern Italy as ports of disembarkation, Mijatović notes that “the goal of ensuring better redistribution of migrants and asylum seekers on the national territory could be achieved by quickly disembarking the rescued people and ensuring that there are alternative practical ways to redistribute them to other parts of the country.”

The Strasbourg-based body, which has nothing to do with European institutions, as has been pointed out, reiterates its call for the government to “suspend cooperation with the Libyan government” to intercept ships at sea, cooperation that was recently renewed.

The Council of Europe is also asking Italy for further investigation into the alleged repatriations of people from Italy to Greece on private vessels, as these people “have been deprived of their liberty in worrying conditions.”

It will be interesting to see where all the protest notes from the organisations as well as official interventions such as this one lead in the case of the Decree, another one in a series of official inhumane decisions taken on behalf of everyone.

#### GREECE
### Missing in reports while alive, but also missing after tragic deaths

_No [mention](https://www.efsyn.gr/ellada/koinonia/376605_exafanizoyn-toys-prosfyges-kai-nekroys) by the authorities of the death of a young woman who came by boat to Mytilene, along with 41 other refugees, in the early hours of last Friday and died tragically from cold and rain in the forest south of the airport._

Not only do the refugees who arrive at the border islands of Greece disappear alive, but even dead, the local Greek media noted in their report on the finding of the body of a young woman who died tragically in the Mytilene airport area.


> “The authorities to this day have not made any reference to the incident of this death, while it is known that for years now all reporting has ceased on the incidents of arrivals which are announced only as total numbers per island, through statistical tables of the Ministry of Migration and Asylum.” 




#### LITHUANIA

Sienos Grupe has compiled a list of 30 people with whom their families have lost contact on the border with Eastern Europe, and their activists are now trying to find them.

This task is made almost impossible by the Lithuanian authorities’ walls-up policy at the borders, reported [**Seebrücke Internationa**](https://twitter.com/Seebruecke_intl) l. Winters in the border region are harsh — in the last few months alone, at least three people on the move have lost their legs to frostbite.


> Many more have suffered injuries to their hands and feet that will accompany them for the rest of their lives. Just as at the Polish-Belarusian border, there are also reports of brutal pushbacks at the Lithuanian-Belarusian border. 





> In January, the Lithuanian government issued a regulation formalising pushbacks as an official procedure. This clearly violates international law! People on the move have the right to be able to apply for asylum. This right is being taken away from them by the pushbacks.

#### UK

The Refugee Council has published a new report showing that most of those crossing the Channel would be given asylum — and why Government plans to detain and deport refugees are inhumane, unworkable and unfair.
- **More than 25,000 who crossed in 2022 allowed to stay as refugees**
- **Four in 10 from five countries with grant rates of over 90% or 80%**
- **Main safe routes cut by 75 per cent and 36 per cent respectively**
- **Latest government plans would lead to tens of thousands facing detention, left in limbo at huge cost**


Find the entire report [**here**](https://www.refugeecouncil.org.uk/latest/news/majority-of-people-on-small-boats-crossing-channel-last-year-are-refugees-new-analysis-shows/) .
### Legal aid for asylum seekers is broken

The system of legal aid for asylum seekers in the UK is broken. The legal advice and representation available is becoming so inadequate that it may breach the state’s human rights obligations and will inevitably lead to significant miscarriages of justice.

The underlying problem is that legal aid no longer provides a sustainable income for qualified legal providers and advocates, meaning that more and more providers are simply giving up. Large charities run by the Immigration Advisory Service and Refugee Legal Centre went bust a decade ago because they couldn’t pay their bills on legal aid. The current haemorrhaging of lawyers out of the sector is more of a slow burn, but it is very real, [reports](https://freemovement.org.uk/legal-aid/) Free Movement.
