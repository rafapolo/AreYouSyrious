### AYS News Digest 30/01/23: Italian government seeks stronger co-operation with Libya

Italian PM Georgia Meloni visited Tripoli to discuss oil, gas and migration // 237 survivors have finally touched the ground of La Spezia Port // News about the Greek legal process against the ERCI SAR volunteers // Access restrictions for NGOs in Greek Ritsona camp // And more

#### LIBYA- ITALY
### The migration issue remains a priority for the Libyan and Italian governments during last Saturday’s meeting in Tripoli

Last Saturday, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni met with the Libyan government in Tripoli. On that occasion, the two countries’ oil companies signed a gas deal worth $8 billion, which AP News reports is the largest single investment in the Libyan energy sector in more than two decades. ( [Read more here)](https://apnews.com/article/politics-libya-government-italy-tripoli-082b5c0ded65b84e63a3df8442868b9c?fbclid=IwAR0-fkfHK_I4fM8KqaEi6OHy544Xu9NCHWjqTHkbyOx5YpRWZj8OCeEKokY)

But one thing is certain — at the centre of the debate and meeting was not only oil and gas. Indeed, economic agreements between the two countries go hand in hand with ever closer co-operation on migration issues. Indeed, Interior Minister Piantendosi was present during the meeting.


> “Libya is clearly for us a strategic economic partner,” 




stated PM Giorgia Meloni. Strategic ally. But not only on the economic front. For years, Italy and with it the EU have been directly responsible for repatriating migrants to Libya. Italy in fact supports the Libyan authorities economically and has recently renewed the Italy-Libya memorandum. During last Saturday’s meeting, the Libyan government stated that Italy would provide ‘fully equipped’ boats to the Libyan coastguard to help stem the flow of migrants to European shores.

For years now, Italian governments have been pursuing a policy of co-operation with Libya, which is meanwhile accused and known for torture and human rights violations of migrants. Whether left-wing or right-wing governments, in Italy the border of the ‘sea’ has been externalised. But now the situation becomes, if possible, even more complicated. The lives of people crossing the Mediterranean are explicitly considered unimportant. Italy’s policies are in fact attacking rescue NGOs, as well as hindering rescue operations. Meanwhile people wait in the hope of not dying at sea. After days at sea last Sunday 237 people were finally allowed to disembark. They have finally touched the ground after an unnecessary journey to La Spezia.

#### SEA / SEARCH and RESCUE
### Some charges in the ERCI case have been dropped, but the seriousness of the process remains

The Greek trial of the ERCI case (named after the organisation of the same name involved, ‘Emergency Response Centre International’) continues. At the moment, the main risk seems to be that the charges, and their implied threats against ERCI volunteers including Sean Bínder and Sara Mardini, may stand until or unless they expire under the Greek statute of limitations.

On 13th January some of the charges were filed. This is on the one hand a victory for Free Humanitarians, who declared:


> “After over 4 years, judges agreed that most defendants will not stand trial and that the charges will be dropped due to the procedural errors that the case has been littered with.” 





However, on the other hand, the severity of the trial itself remains a harsh attack on humanitarian aid. [Last June](https://srdefenders.org/statement-on-preliminary-observations-and-recommendations-following-official-visit-to-greece/) , the UN Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights defenders had already expressed an opinion on this type of criminalisation…


> Putting human rights defenders on trial undermines their work (…) The mere fact of a criminal prosecution and judicial proceedings is a heavy burden for human rights defenders and acts as a deterrent to others 



#### GREECE
### Access restrictions for NGOs in Ritsona camp

Psychosocial support activities are very important for people waiting in limbo, as life in a refugee camp certainly is. However, the NGO Lighthouse Relief has announced that they have been denied access to Ritsona camp, north of Athens. The access to the camp has been blocked after seven years of Lighthouse Relief being present there: no explanation for this restriction has been given yet.
