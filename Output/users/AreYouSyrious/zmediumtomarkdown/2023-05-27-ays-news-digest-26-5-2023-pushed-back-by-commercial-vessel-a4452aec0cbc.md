### Pushed back by commercial vessel— extraordinary events at sea

Illegal pushbacks in the Mediterranean are common, yet two events this week demonstrate the horrors on a different scale.

500 The news of 500 people on one vessel being illegally returned to Libya is extraordinary. Alarm Phone were [first alerted](https://twitter.com/alarm_phone/status/1661069432041422858?fbclid=IwAR3hFyARv24v3pGPi_gQ6VLjHtgkg-MT7HXHJssHwEGiuTd4VjHbbTOzAMc) to the group in distress on Tuesday 25th May, when passengers contacted them to report being stranded with a broken engine days after first departing. By Thursday 27th May, the vessel couldn’t be found by Sea-Watch’s observation plane. The last known position of the boat was in the Maltese SAR zone, yet the Maltese authorities refused to answer calls from NGO rescue ships.

It was later confirmed that the entire group, including women and children, had been returned to Libya. The fact that the group were in European waters was clear, yet the perpetrator of the illegal pushback is unknown.

The other exceptional event was that a pushback was conducted by a commercial vessel. The oil tanker P LONG BEACH responded to a group of 27 people in distress before sailing to Libya. The rescue ship Humanity 1 also responded to the distress call and was in contact with the crew of the tanker — this audio recording proves that the group were illegally taken to Libya, breaking international law.
