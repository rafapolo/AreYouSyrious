#### TUNISIA
### Tunisia is not a safe country, organisations claim

Hate against sub-Saharan people and people on the move in general. Attacks on political opponents and lack of freedom of expression. The recent developments in Tunisia are making it unsafe to live in the country for many people who try to reach Europe by sea. [We have written more about this situation here](ays-news-digest-10-04-2023-400-people-waiting-for-rescue-for-days-in-the-mediterranean-e814e4dc6997) .


> “The undersigned organisations recall that Tunisia is not a so-called safe country of origin for Tunisian citizens. Furthermore, it is not a place of safety for people from sub-Saharan Africa, Tunisians, and other foreigners transiting through this country. We ask the authorities of the European Union and its member states to stop their cooperation with Tunisian authorities on migration control. We also ask to stop their financial and technical support to the Tunisian Coast Guard, and provide safe routes for all.” 




In this way ended a statement signed by many organisations. Nevertheless the political response seems to be going in other directions. After the number of disembarkations of the previous week, Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni declared a state of emergency. At the same time, Manfred Weber, the chairman of EPP (European People’s Party), [is calling for negotiations between the EU and Tunisia](https://globeecho.com/news/europe/germany/weber-eu-sleepwalks-into-new-migration-crisis/?fbclid=IwAR3nPSSfxkpCPfATLiJ4xBgnmv3jMSsbSCg6ce-kw2lfq5L9MuAR1SvGM6Q) on a pact based on the model of the EU-Turkey agreement. As at many EU borders, the fight against “smuggling” and “trafficking” is taken as an excuse to militarise and externalise the border, forcing people to take more dangerous paths. Indeed, Weber stated:


> “Similar to the Turkey agreement, the trafficking gangs must be stopped together” 





For years Tunisia has received funds from the EU in order to prevent “irregular” migration. In the Action Plan for the Central Mediterranean the European Commission state that their aim is to:


> “strengthen capacities of Tunisia \[…\] to prevent irregular departures \[and\] support more effective border and migration management” 





This leaves a terrible choice for people who need to leave Tunisia: remain in a country that is unsafe for them or risk their lives in the journey to Europe.

#### NETHERLANDS
### More unaccompanied minors arrived to Netherlands last year

The arrivals of unaccompanied minors in the Netherlands increased over the past year (doubling from 2,191 in 2021 to 4,207 last year).

Asylum minister Eric van der Burg reacted to these numbers suspending family reunification: a measure that was immediately suspended after the Council of State ruled that it contradicted local and European law.

Children on the move often start their journey to Europe hoping to give an opportunity to their families to travel in a safe way with visas. In fact, the hierarchical passport and visa system make it impossible for many families to travel together and in a regular way.

[Read more here](https://www.dutchnews.nl/news/2023/04/concern-over-child-refugees-after-record-numbers-arrive-in-2022/?fbclid=IwAR1VSPf2Fb-O5FZreDxZkX-xxd9S7e1x7UiBQ3eIQyL0QAZV3xLyHFlcmM0)
#### UK
### The asylum system in the UK shows its criticalities

While many Sudanese asylum seekers are waiting for a decision about their lives, many others are still traveling along unsafe routes. And the UK is welcoming them with hate propaganda.

However, as proved by the current events in Sudan, the country is not safe and people are forced to leave irregularly to find a better place where they can stay.

Indeed there are [no legal routes](https://twitter.com/alison_phipps/status/1647497415920893952?fbclid=IwAR1EOEcEJWc_18WsKxvCNVwSzAXv2XRdSjVbqwEu6slsD5bz67wm7nWRRWU) for people stuck in conflict to reach the UK.

The UK asylum system is showing its holes from many points of view.

Not only that people coming from dangerous countries are still waiting for an answer, but many vulnerable asylum seekers and refugees live with the constant fear of deportation. This fear spread amongst minors after police collected asylum-seeking children’s biometric data. Children’s mobile phones were also checked by the authorities.

These actions were conducted under Operation Innerste, a multi-agency safeguarding initiative developed in 2017, to stop unaccompanied migrant children being trafficked from Home Office hotels.

However, Benny Hunter, worker for the rights of children asylum-seekers, said:


> “there are legitimate concerns about what this ‘safeguarding’ involves, when some police forces are prosecuting illegal entry and the Home Office is collecting data from these encounters whilst seeking to undermine the right to claim asylum.” 




Read more about the investigation:


[![](https://libertyinvestigates.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/K3NG4F-scaled.jpg)](https://libertyinvestigates.org.uk/articles/deportation-fears-after-police-collect-asylum-seeking-childrens-biometric-data/?fbclid=IwAR28MfMLC3brRzxndBUoU8TfC7Zn2VFDOFW2wqe3UiC_4gXsKRU7L9xcPLg)


The UK government is devoting efforts and funds both to prevent arrivals by the Channel and to return people to the countries of origin or to third countries. In the meantime other previous plans on migration management are failing, as is the Rwanda plan. The founder of Care4Calais, Clare Moseley, stated:


> “The Rwanda policy devastates refugees’ lives while being unworkable and ineffective. It’s time the government stopped wasting time and money and looked at solutions that have a realistic chance of success.” 





Indeed between April and October 2022, 6,200 notices of intent were issued with the threat of deportation to Rwanda, but only seven people were formally declared inadmissible for asylum in the UK. Six were deported, while almost 3,200 asylum seekers were admitted into the UK asylum process.

Moreover Rwanda isn't suitable to host asylum seekers and this has already been denounced many times.
